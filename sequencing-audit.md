# Curriculum Sequencing Audit

Each entry lists the source file, concepts introduced, prior knowledge assumed, and every question in the order a student encounters it. INSTRUCT step questions and their follow-ups are listed together. Practice phases are labeled P (generated items, not fixed questions).

---

## 1. lesson-one.html — Equal Relations
*Source: `lesson-one-specific.js`*

**Introduces:**
- Equality as a relation name for two same-size bars
- Two visual formats that show equality: stacked bars and dotted-line bars
- The rule that the picture must show equality — visual evidence is required

**Assumes:** Nothing. This is the foundational lesson.

**Questions:**
1. "What is the relation?" — Recognizes "equal" as the name when two bars are the same size
2. "What do we call two bars that are the same size?" — Recalls the label from Q1
3. "Are these bars equal or not equal?" — Distinguishes equal from not-equal given two different-sized bars
4. "What do we call two bars that are different sizes?" — Recalls the not-equal label
5. "Can I call these bars equal?" — Understands that close-in-size is not enough; visual proof is required
6. "What must the picture show to say two bars are equal?" — Recalls the stacked-or-dotted-lines rule
7. "Are these bars equal or not equal?" *(dotted-line format)* — Applies the equality rule to the second visual format
8. "How does the picture show equality here?" — Explains what dotted lines represent
9. P — Identification practice: "Equal or Not Equal?" — Classifies randomly generated models as equal or not
10. P — Naming practice: "Name the relation." — Constructs "[color] equals unknown" from a model with one bar marked as unknown

## 2. lesson-two.html — Part-Whole Relations
*Source: `lesson-two-specific.js`*

**Introduces:**
- Part-whole structure: two part bars together equal one whole bar
- Labels: whole (top/largest bar), parts (two smaller bars below)
- Plus finds the whole (part + part = whole)
- Minus finds a missing part (whole − known part = unknown part)

**Assumes:** L1 equal relations (a review phase confirms mastery before new content begins)

**Questions:**
1. R — Review: "Equal or Not Equal?" — Confirms L1 mastery with mixed equal/not-equal models
2. "What color is the whole?" — Identifies which bar plays the whole role
3. "What colors are the two parts?" — Identifies the two part bars
4. "If the [color] part is removed, what remains?" *(faded-part visual)* — Connects part removal to the minus operation
5. "What word do we use when we take away?" — Recalls minus as the operation for part removal
6. "What do we use to find the whole?" — Links plus to whole-finding
7. "What do we use to find a missing part?" — Links minus to part-finding
8. P — Identification practice: "Part-Whole or Not?" — Classifies models; non-part-whole distractors include misaligned sizes, solo bar, and two bars with no whole
9. P — Naming practice: "Name the relation." — Constructs the correct relation based on which role is unknown (whole → plus; part → minus)

## 3. lesson-three.html — Comparisons & Differences
*Source: `lesson-three-specific.js`*

**Introduces:**
- More and less as labels for unequal bars in a comparison
- The difference bar: a visual gap showing how much more the larger bar is
- Three-bar algebra: larger = smaller + difference (any one of the three can be found from the other two)
- Connection: difference is itself a part in a hidden part-whole within the comparison

**Assumes:** L2 part-whole relations (review phase confirms mastery)

**Questions:**
1. R — Review: "Part-Whole or Not?" — Confirms L2 mastery with valid and invalid part-whole models
2. "Which bar is less?" — Applies more/less terminology to identify the smaller bar in a comparison
3. "If [color] is less — what is [color]?" — Derives the complementary label (more) from knowing the other
4. "What do we call the faded gap?" — Names the difference bar
5. "What relation does smaller + difference = larger show?" — Connects the comparison structure to part-whole
6. "What equals the [color] difference?" — Applies three-bar algebra to find the difference
7. "What equals the [color] smaller bar?" — Applies three-bar algebra in a different direction
8. P — Identification practice: "Comparison or Not?" — Distinguishes comparison models from part-whole distractors
9. P — Naming practice: "Name the relation." — Constructs the correct relation based on unknown role (larger → plus; smaller or difference → minus)

## 4. lesson-four.html — Fractional Relations
*Source: `lesson-four-specific.js`*

**Introduces:**
- A whole divided into equal parts (halves, thirds, fourths, fifths)
- Fraction naming based on count: 2 equal parts = halves, 3 = thirds, 4 = fourths
- The fractional relation form: "one [fraction] of [whole color] equals [part color]"
- Visual format: whole bar on top, equal-width boxes below

**Assumes:** L3 comparison relations (review phase confirms mastery)

**Questions:**
1. R — Review: "Comparison or Not?" — Confirms L3 mastery with valid and invalid comparison models
2. "How many halves are in one whole?" — Counts parts and establishes the denominator-to-name link for halves
3. "What do we call two equal parts of a whole?" — Recalls the fraction name for 2 parts
4. "What do we call each part when a whole is split into three equal parts?" — Extends fraction naming to thirds
5. "What is the filled box equal to?" *(thirds)* — Applies the "one third of whole" relation
6. "What is the filled box equal to?" *(fourths)* — Applies the relation to a new denominator
7. "How many fourths are in one whole?" — Reinforces counting equal parts to determine the denominator
8. P — Identification practice: "Fractional Relation or Not?" — Classifies models; distractors include unequal boxes, mismatched box counts, and no whole bar
9. P — Naming practice: "Name the relation." — Counts equal parts, identifies the fraction name, and constructs "one [fraction] of [whole] equals unknown"

## 5. lesson-five.html — Multiplicative Relations
*Source: `lesson-five-specific.js`*

**Introduces:**
- The multiplicative direction: the whole is N times the part (inverse of fractional)
- Dual naming: the same picture has two valid relation names — fractional and multiplicative
- Flexible solving: either relation can be used to find an unknown

**Assumes:** L4 fractional relations (review phase confirms mastery via button-choice naming)

**Questions:**
1. R — Review: "Name the fractional relation." — Confirms L4 mastery; student selects the correct "one [fraction] of [whole] equals [part]" from three options
2. "[Color] is how many times [color]?" — Reads the picture in the multiplicative direction (whole = N × part)
3. "If a whole had three equal parts, the whole would be how many times the part?" — Generalizes the count-to-times rule beyond the specific picture shown
4. "[Color] is how many times [color]?" *(different denominator)* — Applies the multiplicative naming format to a new case
5. Name the fractional relation for the same picture — Practices naming both directions from one model
6. "Name a true relation between [color] and [color]." — Accepts either fractional or multiplicative; tests that student recognizes both as valid
7. "Now name the other direction." — Requires naming the direction not just given; confirms dual-direction fluency
8. P — Naming practice: "Name the relation." — Constructs either fractional or multiplicative relation depending on unknown role; both correct forms are accepted
9. P — Free naming practice: "Name a true relation." — Names any valid relation for an unmarked model; either direction accepted

## 6. lesson-compound.html — Compound Relations
*Source: `lesson-compound-specific.js`*

**Introduces:**
- Compound relations: two linked models connected by one shared bar (the bridge)
- Bridge concept: solving the first model reveals a bar that was unknown, making it usable in the second model
- Strategy: always solve the model with one unknown first; it unlocks the second

**Assumes:** All five simple relations (L1–L5); no review phase — full mastery assumed

**Questions:**
1. "What equals [color]?" *(single model, any simple relation type)* — Confirms that all five simple relation types can be named before compound work begins
2. "What do you need to find two unknowns?" — Surfaces that a single relation cannot handle two unknowns, motivating the compound approach
3. "Which model can you solve first — top or bottom?" — Identifies which of the two models has only one unknown and is therefore solvable
4. "Why must you solve the one-unknown model first?" — Articulates the solvability rule
5. "What equals the unknown in the [position] model?" *(first solve)* — Names the relation for the solvable model; finding this unknown reveals the bridge bar
6. "How many unknowns are left?" *(after bridge is found)* — Confirms that solving the first model reduced the second model to one unknown
7. "How many unknowns are left now?" *(bridge revealed in second model)* — Re-counts to confirm the second model is now solvable
8. "Which model do you use now?" — Directs focus to the second model now that the bridge is known
9. "What equals the unknown in the [position] model?" *(second solve)* — Names the relation for the now-solvable second model
10. "What is a compound relation?" — Synthesizes the definition: two linked relations, each finding one unknown through a shared bridge
11. "Which model do you solve first?" *(full integrated problem)* — Applies the complete strategy from the start on a fresh model
12. "What equals the unknown in the [position] model?" *(second solve on full problem)* — Completes the second step of the integrated problem
13. P — Naming practice (two-phase word bank): Build the relation for the first model using a word bank → bridge is revealed → build the relation for the second model; tests sequential solving and bridge-based unlocking

## 7. lesson-unit3.html — Complex Relations
*Source: embedded JS + `lesson-unit3-specific.js`; Lesson 3.1*

**Introduces:**
- Complex (two-step) visual relations: a part-whole step feeding into a fractional step
- The bridge as a color that appears in both steps, connecting them
- Strategy: identify which step has the unknown, solve it, then use the bridge in the other step

**Assumes:** All simple relations (L1–L5) and compound relations (Lesson 2.1)

**Questions:**
1. "What is the bridge between the two steps?" — Identifies the color that appears in both steps
2. "Why is [color] called the bridge?" — Explains that the bridge connects the two steps by appearing in both
3. "What equals the unknown in the first step?" — Names the part-whole relation for step 1 (operation depends on which role is unknown: whole → plus; part → minus)
4. "What does solving the first step unlock?" — Understands that finding the bridge makes step 2 solvable
5. "What equals the unknown [color] part?" — Names the fractional relation for step 2 ("one [fraction] of [bridge] equals unknown")
6. *(Implicit follow-up)* Count the equal parts to verify the fraction name — Reinforces denominator-to-fraction-name mapping
7. "Which step has the unknown?" *(full two-step problem)* — Identifies which step to address first
8. "Name the relation to find the unknown." — Names the correct relation for the identified step
9. P — Identification practice: "First step or Second step?" — Given a two-step model, identifies which step contains the unknown
10. P — Naming practice: Build the relation using word buttons — Constructs the full part-whole or fractional relation for the step containing the unknown

## 8. lesson-unit4.html — Word Problems: Simple Relations
*Standalone file; Lesson 4.1*

**Introduces:**
- Translating word problem text into a bar model
- Translating a bar model into a named relation
- All five simple relation types encountered in narrative form (no numbers)

**Assumes:** All five simple relations (L1–L5); bar model structure and relation naming

**Questions:**
1. "What is the relation?" *(Part-Whole, unknown = whole; Tom's stickers)* — Reads narrative, recognizes part-whole structure, names "red stickers + blue stickers = whole collection"
2. "What is the relation?" *(Part-Whole, unknown = part; rope cut into pieces)* — Reads narrative, identifies that a part is unknown, names "whole rope − short piece = long piece"
3. "What is the relation?" *(Comparison, unknown = difference; Ana & Ben jumping)* — Reads narrative, identifies comparison structure, names "Ben's jump + difference = Ana's jump"
4. P — Draw practice: Construct the bar model from a word problem — Evaluated for correct bar count, labels, and proportions matching the problem
5. P — Name practice: Build the relation from a word problem using word bank buttons — Constructs the correct relation tokens for the model just drawn

## 9. lesson-unit4b.html — Word Problems with Explicit Unknowns
*Standalone file; Lesson 4.2*

**Introduces:**
- Explicit "?" labeling on bar models: the unknown quantity is marked with a question mark
- Unknowns in different positions across different relation types

**Assumes:** All five simple relations; bar model drawing introduced in Lesson 4.1

**Questions:**
1. "Where is the unknown?" *(Comparison, unknown = difference; Jenny & Elsa dominoes)* — Identifies the difference as the unknown quantity and marks it with ?
2. "Where is the unknown?" *(Part-Whole, unknown = whole; board split into sections)* — Identifies the whole as the unknown and marks it with ?
3. "Where is the unknown?" *(Multiplicative, unknown = whole; long rope four times short rope)* — Identifies the whole in a multiplicative context and marks it with ?
4. P — Draw practice: Construct the bar model from a word problem and place the ? label on the correct bar — Evaluated for bar count, labels, correct ? placement, and proportions

## 10. lesson-unit5.html — Complex Word Problems
*Standalone file; Lesson 4.3*

**Introduces:**
- Two-step word problems in narrative form
- Identifying the bridge quantity from problem text
- Drawing a two-step bar model from text
- Naming the step-2 fractional relation after the bridge is established by step 1

**Assumes:** Complex (two-step) relation structure from Lesson 3.1; bar model drawing from Lessons 4.1 and 4.2

**Questions:**
1. "Name the relation for step 2." *(halves; Sam's tokens — red + blue = whole, whole ÷ 2 = unknown group)* — Identifies the bridge (whole collection), counts 2 equal parts, names "one half of whole collection equals unknown"
2. "Name the relation for step 2." *(thirds; garden sections — vegetable + flower = whole, whole ÷ 3 = unknown portion)* — Extends fractional naming to thirds
3. "Name the relation for step 2." *(fourths; stone pile — small + large = whole, whole ÷ 4 = unknown portion)* — Extends fractional naming to fourths
4. P — Draw practice: Construct the two-step bar model from a word problem — Evaluated for correct two-bar step-1 structure, labels, and proportions
5. P — Name practice: Build the step-2 fractional relation using word bank buttons — Constructs "one [fraction] of [bridge] equals unknown" from the model just drawn
